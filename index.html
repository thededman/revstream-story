The idea

Every month, the same rebuild, by hand.

The lab's revenue lived in one large Excel master file, fed every day with exports from the lab system and the outsourced billing service. Each sheet was kept in step by hand: cases, CPT lines, payments, payers, the fee schedule.

The billing lead knew exactly what the month-end reports should say. Getting there meant rebuilding them from scratch every month with COUNTIFS and SUMIFS, then checking the bank deposits one line at a time.

The question was simple: could the lab have a system that already knows all this?

  • Pasting daily exports into the master file, and catching the ones pasted twice.
  • Pricing every CPT line against the right fee schedule, payer by payer.
  • Matching hundreds of bank deposits to posted payments, by hand.
  • Rebuilding the month's revenue tables with formulas, for every close.
  • Counting each pathologist's sign-outs to prepare their monthly invoice.
  • Answering "where did this number come from?" with no audit trail.

Timeline · 2026

Fourteen weeks, meeting by meeting.

Almost every step below began as a meeting with the billing lead in the morning and shipped as a tested release the same day or the next.

  1. Jul 8
    The first version

    A web app that reads the master file and replaces the spreadsheet's cash-flow workflow. Demoed the next day; the feedback shipped that week.

  2. Aug
    Hardened to run in-house

    Moved onto the company's own server: two-factor sign-in, encrypted connections only, nightly backups.

  3. Sep 11
    Live

    The billing team switches from the master file to RevStream: daily uploads, revenue by CPT line, priced from the fee schedule.

  4. Sep 30
    A Test system beside the real one

    A full copy where every change is tried on real data before it goes live, chosen at sign-in.

  5. Oct 1–2
    Payments and bank deposits

    Payments flow in from the billing exports; the bank's deposit log is matched to them, check by check.

  6. Oct 6
    Audit trail and read-only access

    Who changed what, on every case. Read-only accounts for people who only need the reports.

  7. Oct 8–9
    The month-end close, automated

    The revenue report the billing lead built by hand each month, now laid out by the system and matching the hand-built one to the cent. One click closes the month and produces each pathologist's invoice.

  8. Oct 12
    Reconciliation

    Every difference between the bank and the books explained, case by case and check by check, then accepted with a reason until the month reads Reconciled.

  9. Nov 1
    Full go-live, planned

    The whole monthly cycle runs in RevStream: uploads, revenue, payments, bank reconciliation, close and invoices.

How it was built

One engineer, Claude, and the people who do the work.

RevStream was built by the lab's IT engineer working with Claude, Anthropic's AI model, as a pair: the billing team set the direction in regular meetings, and each request became a tested release.

Meet

The billing lead walks through what's wrong, or what the next report needs to say.

Trace

The numbers are traced in the real files first, to the cent, so the fix answers the real question.

Build and test

The change is built with tests on made-up data, and checked in a browser in light and dark.

Test, then live

It goes to the Test system for the billing team to try, then to the live system.

  • No real patient data in tests, screenshots or anything shared.
  • Every release recorded in a changelog, in plain words.
  • Every import backed up first, and undoable.
  • Every hour logged, so the cost is known, not guessed.

What's next

Bringing billing itself in-house.

Pending · clearinghouse setup

Today the lab's claims are billed by an outside service, and RevStream reads its exports. The next phase sends claims from RevStream through a clearinghouse and reads the insurers' remittances back, so payments post themselves. That would replace the outside billing service entirely.

Case signed off→Claim built in RevStream→Clearinghouse→Insurer→Remittance back→Payment posted

A proof of concept already builds and checks claims on made-up data. It waits on the clearinghouse account. Alongside it: sales commission reports, and invoices sent to accounts payable straight from the closed month.

RevStream is an internal system; this page tells its story. No patient data, case numbers or company financial figures appear here, and names are left out on purpose.

Built by David Dempster with Claude. Django, PostgreSQL, Docker; two-factor sign-in through Duo.
